# Ship 30 for 30 Essay Skill

## Purpose
Create a useful, focused online essay from the current conversation and retrieved transcript evidence. This is a writing workflow, not permission to invent facts. The assignment requires approximately 1,250 words, a strong hook, clear narrative progression, skimmable headings/bullets, selective bold emphasis, a specific takeaway, and claims grounded in the transcript knowledge base.

## Inputs
- User's topic or question.
- Relevant retrieved transcript passages.
- Source metadata: guest, episode title, source URL, and timestamp if available.
- Intended audience or practical problem, if specified.

If the retrieved evidence is missing or insufficient, explain the gap and ask for a narrower topic or retrieve additional evidence through the approved retrieval tool. Do not manufacture citations.

## Writing principles
1. **One clear idea:** express the central argument in one sentence before drafting.
2. **Specific audience:** write for a product manager, founder, or growth practitioner trying to solve a concrete problem.
3. **Strong hook:** open with a sharp observation, tension, counterintuitive claim, short scenario, or question. Do not fabricate a personal anecdote or quote.
4. **Promise value early:** establish what the reader will understand or be able to do by the end.
5. **Narrative progression:** move logically from problem/context to insight, explanation, evidence, application, and takeaway.
6. **Evidence before certainty:** connect factual claims to retrieved passages. Attribute opinions and disagreements to the relevant guest rather than presenting them as universal truth.
7. **Concrete details:** use specific examples only when supported by the evidence or clearly label them as hypothetical.
8. **Readable structure:** use descriptive headings, short paragraphs, bullets where they improve scanning, and selective bold emphasis. Avoid excessive heading fragmentation.
9. **Useful synthesis:** do more than concatenate summaries. Explain relationships between ideas, limits, and practical implications without overstating the source.
10. **Actionable ending:** provide a specific next step, checklist, or decision framework grounded in the material.
11. **Honest limitations:** identify disagreement, uncertainty, or missing evidence when material to the argument.
12. **No filler:** every section should advance the central idea.

## Workflow
### Step 1 — Evidence map
List the relevant source passages and what each supports. Identify conflicting advice, evidence gaps, and claims that should not be made.

### Step 2 — Choose the angle
Write:
- Intended reader
- Reader's problem
- Central thesis
- Why it matters now to that reader
- Practical result the reader should leave with

If evidence does not support a defensible thesis, stop and report the gap.

### Step 3 — Build an outline
Create a narrative outline:
- Hook and problem
- Context and why common approaches fail (only if supported)
- Two to four main insights or stages
- Evidence/examples for each
- Practical application
- Specific takeaway

Use the smallest number of sections that creates a clear progression.

### Step 4 — Draft
Aim for approximately 1,250 words. A reasonable range is 1,100–1,400 unless the user asks otherwise. Use a strong opening, coherent transitions, scannable formatting, and a specific takeaway.

### Step 5 — Ground claims
For each factual claim about what a guest said or what happened:
- Ensure a retrieved source supports it.
- Preserve source links and episode/guest metadata.
- Use direct quotations only when the source text supports the exact wording.
- Never invent timestamps or imply a paraphrase is a verbatim quote.
- When sources disagree, present the difference fairly.

### Step 6 — Edit
Check:
- Does the hook create interest without clickbait?
- Is the thesis clear?
- Does each section follow from the previous one?
- Are the examples source-supported?
- Is the writing easy to scan?
- Is there a specific takeaway?
- Are there repetitive or unsupported claims?
- Is the result near the target length?

## Output format
1. Title.
2. Essay with a strong opening and descriptive headings.
3. Specific takeaway section.
4. Sources used: episode title, guest, source URL, timestamp when available.
5. Brief note if the indexed evidence is incomplete or conflicting.

Do not output internal reasoning or an evidence map unless requested. Keep citations visible in the final essay in a readable form.
